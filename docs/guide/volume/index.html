Settings › Volume Controls

A ceiling, a wake-up level, and a Mute that does what you want

Four settings that between them decide how loud your receiver can ever get, what it does when it wakes, and how far Mute drops it. They live on the receiver, so they hold whether you use the app, the physical remote or the front panel.

Where it lives

Settings › Volume Controls

Open Settings and scroll to Volume Controls - "Volume limit, power-on level, and mute depth". Everything on this page is written straight to the receiver, so it survives closing the app and applies to every way you control it.

One at a time

What each setting does

Volume Scale

How the volume reads, both on screen and on the receiver's own display. 0 - 98 is the relative scale most people know; -79.5 to 18.0 dB is the absolute scale, where 0.0 dB is reference level. If you have ever seen a review quote "-15 dB" and wondered how that maps to your dial, this is the switch that answers it.

Volume Limit

A hard ceiling. Once set, nothing goes past it - not the app, not the physical remote, not the front-panel dial, because the limit lives on the receiver rather than in the app. The obvious use is protecting speakers or a room full of people from a mis-tap, and it is the setting to reach for before handing the remote to a house guest. Off removes the cap.

Power-On Level

Use last volume is on by default: the receiver wakes to whatever it was at when you switched it off. Turn it off and you can pin a fixed level instead, so a late session at high volume never becomes an unpleasant surprise the next morning.

Mute Level

How far Mute drops the volume. Full silences it. -40 dB and -20 dB attenuate instead, which is the better choice when you want to hold a conversation over a film without losing your place in it.

Elsewhere in the app

The controls you use every day

  • The Home dashboard carries the master volume, mute and power for the main zone.
  • The Remote tab gives you a volume rocker beside the D-pad and transport keys, with the level shown as you go. Remote guide →
  • Your phone's hardware volume keys drive the receiver while the app is in front, so you can adjust without looking.
  • Each zone has its own volume, independent of the main one. Zones guide →
  • Scenes can capture Master Volume, so recalling one sets the level along with everything else. Scenes guide →
  • The on-screen volume readout on your TV can be moved or switched off entirely. On-Screen Display guide →
Volume changes are paced deliberately rather than fired one per pixel, so holding the rocker or sweeping the slider ramps smoothly instead of flooding the receiver with commands it would queue up and apply late.
Common questions

Volume FAQ

Does the volume limit apply to the physical remote too?
Yes. The limit is a receiver setting, not an app setting, so it caps every route: the app, the bundled remote, the front-panel dial and any other controller on your network.
Do these settings apply to Zone 2 and Zone 3?
Volume Controls covers the main zone. Each zone carries its own volume, and its own power and input, on the Zones tab.
Why does the app show 0 to 98 when reviews quote decibels?
They are two views of the same dial. Switch Volume Scale to -79.5 to 18.0 dB and the readout matches the way calibration levels are normally quoted, where 0.0 dB is reference.
Can I use my phone's volume buttons?
Yes. While AVR Maestro is in the foreground, your phone's hardware volume keys drive the receiver directly.
